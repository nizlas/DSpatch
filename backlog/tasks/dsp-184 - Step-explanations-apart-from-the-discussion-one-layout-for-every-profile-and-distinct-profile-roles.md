## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Niclas's evaluation of 0.1.11 on Linux, 9 November 2026 (points 1, 3, 5 and the explanation side of 4).

Verified problem: switching profile works in Step, where saved explanations can be switched between, but generating a profile explanation also puts its request and answer in Discussion, which fills the chat with copies of explanations; read from Discussion, the mixture looked as if the profile switch did not work. The earlier diagnosis that the switch itself failed was wrong and is not built on.

Decided behaviour (Niclas, 9 November 2026; replaces VISION 3.4's "asked through the review discussion … and continued in the discussion" for step deepenings):
- Profile switch and regeneration update only the step's explanation in Step and create no discussion entries.
- Each profile has its own saved explanations and its own explanation history per review, walk and step. Switching back shows the existing text at once without a model call. The walk's original text is the starting point for the profile it was generated in and can always be returned to.
- Tell me more and Explain more simply from Step work within the chosen profile and are saved there; the previous explanation can be returned to.
- Discussion keeps the person's coherent conversation. A profile switch does not change the conversation's style or add messages. When the person discusses the current explanation it can be given as context without being copied in as visible chat entries.
- Earlier automatic profile entries are separated only where they can be identified with certainty; no history is deleted by guessing; earlier conversations are kept.
- The guards stay: no duplicate calls, no late answers on the wrong step or profile, no automatic resending after a failure; a failure needs an explicit new attempt.
- Profiles: Essentials says what the code does and the most important consequence, short and precise. Standard also explains how and why the relevant parts connect, for a developer who knows programming but not this code, adding understanding rather than words and no basic course. Teacher builds the needed concepts, explains the reasoning and uses examples where they help; its length is not cut in general. The instructions are consistent for walk generation, alternative explanations, regeneration and follow-up actions; profile ids and Swedish/English names are not mixed up. Generic product feedback: no example or project-specific name is hard-coded.
- Explanations follow the walk: generation gets the walk's order and what its steps cover; no file names or line numbers in prose when the walk shows the code; "here", "in the next step", "as we saw earlier" where true; no pre-empting later steps' explanations.
- One basic layout and component order for every profile; the profile governs the manner of explanation, not where navigation sits. Step and place navigation stays easy to reach with longer text. Step and Discussion stay clearly apart, and the profile chooser is clearly about the step explanation.
- Addition (Niclas, 9 November 2026): remove the whole "What it asks for" section from the review view — the link, the profile description and the expandable instruction text including "Instruction version". The person chooses an explanation profile and reads the code explanation; they should not meet prompt definitions or internal version information. Not moved to another panel or expandable control in the same flow. Instructions and version metadata stay internal for generation, caching and troubleshooting (the prompt sent is kept with each stored explanation). The chooser is enough in the interface; options may have a short tooltip saying the difference in plain user language. "Show more" stays for deepening the code explanation itself. Niclas clarified the same day that this is a placement decision, not hiding: the instructions stay explicit, versioned and inspectable through the project's intended means (the profile module and each stored explanation's record), as AGENT_BEHAVIOUR_AND_ORCHESTRATION.md section 1 asks; section 2's visible runtime contract concerns the graph editor, not every user flow. Not a departure. No new inspection view is built in this change.
- Correction (Niclas, 9 November 2026, after further evaluation): Essentials is still too detailed — closer to what he meant by Standard — and both retell the code. Essentials is normally about two sentences: the purpose and the most important events at a level above individual statements; decisive conditions included but summarised understandably; concrete identifiers only when they help the reader connect the explanation to the code; mechanical details (indexes, loop bounds, every single call) left out unless decisive; necessary further detail under Show more. Two sentences is a normal target, not a reason to pack seven sentences' content into two long ones. For every profile: the explanation adds understanding beyond what can be read directly in the code and its comments, and does not routinely retell each statement, condition or comment in prose. Standard may develop the connections — why the conditions exist, how the parts work together, which consequences matter — not a line-by-line account; detail is justified where the code is hard to read, the behaviour surprising or a consequence easy to miss, and then the explanation makes exactly that clear. Teacher keeps its length and room for concepts and examples, with the same demand on explanatory value. No invented intentions to make text sound explanatory: what the code shows is kept apart from an interpretation of why it is written so. The test of a text: "What does this help the reader understand that is not already plain from the code or its comment?" If the answer is only that the same details are said in sentences, the text needs rework. (Niclas gave an example of the desired Essentials level from his own work code; it is not recorded here and is not hard-coded anywhere.)
- Quality correction (Niclas, 9 November 2026): a step held three pieces of code and two were treated in passing; even Teacher reduced an acquire function with a mutex, a shared pointer, a flag and a condition variable to "get-or-create" and "safe to repeat". A code place is not treated because it is in the step or its function is named: every central mechanism is explained at the chosen profile's level, and the three places would normally be steps of their own. Teacher identifies the concepts and connections needed to review the code — e.g. which shared state a lock protects and which other paths or threads use the same lock; why lookup and creation must be protected together; a scoped lock's lifetime and automatic release; what shared ownership means for ownership and lifetime, and that shared ownership does not make the contents thread-safe; how a new request, its flag, the notification and the waiting loop that receives it connect; the difference between reusing an object, creating a request and the work actually having been done. The agent uses the shared read access to read the relevant types, callers, accesses to the state and the waiting/working code at the review's revision before writing, and does not guess the architecture from names. Where relevant code really is missing, Teacher still explains the general concepts and keeps them apart from what cannot be established about this implementation; missing context leads neither to invented connections nor to skipped concepts. Purpose and interplay in connected reasoning — not line-by-line paraphrase or a general language lesson; Teacher takes the room understanding needs; Essentials stays short. A synthetic quality case with a producer/worker relation and shared ownership is judged on whether the explanation makes the relation understandable; containing the words is not a test.

## Implementation Notes

<!-- SECTION:NOTES:BEGIN -->
Implementer, 9 November 2026 (uncommitted working tree; DSP-186 worked in the same checkout at the same time).

**Store.** `app/src/shared/step-explanations.ts` (shape, adoption, history rules) and `app/src/main/step-explanation-channel.ts` (writer). `<recordsDir>/<key>.explanations.json` beside `<key>.discussion.json`, same identity key (DSP-186's review id). Format 1: `{ version, identity, entries[], savedAt }`, written whole to `.tmp` and renamed; a newer format is left read-only, an unreadable one is moved aside. An entry: `id, walkId, stepId, basis (stepContentHash), profile, action (present|regenerate|more|simpler|explain), parent (entry it builds on, null = the walk's own text), selection?, text (short + marker + details, references block taken off), references/contextReferences/adjustments/insufficient (checked against Git as for discussion answers), status (sending|streaming|done|failed|cancelled|unknown), error?, at, finishedAt?, prompt (exact text sent; never sent to a window), instructionVersion, language, provider, model, dspatchSessionId, dspatchRunId, codeAccess?, trigger (auto|person)`. Entries still on their way when DSpatch closed read back as `unknown`.

**History.** Per (walk, step, basis, profile): the walk's own text first, then finished entries oldest first; shown = latest unless the person went Back/Forward (window state, not stored; cleared by the person's next action in that slot). Switching profile reads the store; no call.

**Channel separation.** The channel is created by `ReviewDiscussionService` and uses its agent choice, model, adapter factory and policy checks (`openSession`, extracted from `ensureSession`), envelope and read-only code access, prompt builder and reference check: no second SDK integration. It has its own provider session (never in the discussion's session or recap), its own one-at-a-time lock (neither blocks nor is blocked by a discussion question), and its own store. Requests travel on the discussion's IPC path marked `channel: 'explanation'` (preload `explain` / `stopExplanation`), so review-ipc.ts and project-host.ts needed no change; events go on the discussion's event channel as `explanation`, `explanation-delta`, `explanation-busy`. Guards: a duplicate for a slot with one on its way is refused; an answer lands on the entry of its run; an automatic request is refused when the slot's last attempt failed/stopped/ended unknown (only the person's Try again sends it); the window still asks only from the page showing the panel, only for the current step and profile, 500 ms after it settles, never on open or load, and a wish that met the busy lock goes when the channel is free and the person is still there.

**Discussion.** Questions with `deepen.action === 'present'` (the only entries identified with certainty as automatic profile explanations) are hidden with their answers and left out of the recap (`isAutomaticProfileEntry`, `recapFor`); the stored file is not rewritten. Person-initiated older deepenings stay visible. A discussion question carries no profile section; a question about the current step carries the generated explanation shown in Step as a hidden context section (`readingContextSection`; marked `readingContext: true` on the question). A `present` deepening sent to the discussion is refused. "Ask in the discussion…" in Step switches to Discussion and focuses the composer.

**Layout.** Cause of the profile-dependent order: the walk's own text put its summary above the places and its body/why below them, while a generated explanation came whole above the places; Standard (the walks' own profile) therefore read in another order. Now one order for every profile: step title → explanation bar (chooser labelled "Explanation"/"Förklaring", Back/Forward with position, status, Regenerate/Try again/Stop) → places (when more than one) with a sticky place navigation row → explanation (Show more kept) → actions (Tell me more, Explain more simply, Explain this, Ask in the discussion…). "What it asks for", the description paragraph, the instruction text and its version are removed (CSS and i18n too); options carry a short plain tooltip. Before any walk exists the chooser stays in the tab row as "Explanations", the profile walks are prepared in. A walk window shows DSP-186's staleness notice (`generationStale`), never regenerating.

**Profiles (version 4).** One place: `presentation-profiles.ts`. Shared rules added to every base text and to more/simpler/explain: understanding beyond the code and its comments, no invented intentions (interpretation marked), and the self-check. Grounding no longer demands naming every central part or every default. Walk-relative rules (`WALK_WRITING_RULES`) and the walk's outline (`## Where this step is in the walk`, sent by the window) reach walk generation, present, regenerate, more, simpler and explain. Teacher's hard-coded build-system example was removed. Before/after on a neutral example (step "Build the greeting with a default", walk "Greeting a visitor"), profile-specific parts and changed shared lines only:

Before (v3), shared: "Name the concrete functions, settings, types or other parts that are central to the step, with their real names…" / "State the relevant default values explicitly." / length: "…Do not press many clauses into very long sentences."
Before, Essentials: "Select the core and put it precisely: what changes, the behaviour that matters most, and what the reader needs to understand or check here." / "Keep the concrete names, the relevant defaults and the most important behaviour." / "Leave out mechanical intermediate steps and side details…" / "A short lead sentence and a few short bullet points are welcome…" / Length: "About two sentences, or the same said as a few short bullet points".
Before, Standard: "Give a straight technical explanation for a developer of ordinary experience who did not write this change." / "Explain how the central parts connect and the mechanism…: what calls what, what data moves, what decides the outcome." / "Do not assume detailed knowledge of this project…" / Length: "About five sentences".

After (v4), shared: "When you name a function, setting, type or other part, use its real name…" / "State a default value explicitly when it is decisive for what the step does." / "Add understanding beyond what can be read directly in the code and its comments. Do not retell each statement, condition or comment in prose." / "Do not invent intentions to make the text sound explanatory. Keep apart what the code shows and an interpretation of why it is written so, and mark an interpretation as one." / "Before you answer, ask: what does this help the reader understand that is not already plain from the code or its comments? If the answer is only 'the same details, said in sentences', rewrite it." / length: "…Do not press the content of many sentences into a few long ones: a shorter profile says less, at a higher level, not the same in denser sentences."
