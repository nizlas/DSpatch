## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Niclas's assignment of 10 November 2026, after delegating a walk to the agent in 0.1.17. The Step panel's top filled with the review report and its findings, all open, some headings wrapped to a word a line, closing one finding seemed to affect others, the report stayed and took room while he moved between the walk's steps, and the Gerrit (diff) view showed every finding open. The result read "374/420 changed lines reviewed and approved by the agent" and "46 changed lines not approved · 4 open questions · 3 verification gaps", which mixes the agent's material with the person's decision.

**Niclas's decision (10 November 2026).** A delegated agent review gives basis and recommendations; it does not accept or reject the code. The agent reports what it examined, its findings, questions and verification gaps, and its assessment with its grounds and uncertainty. The person accepts or rejects. Delegation, a finished run or a positive assessment is never recorded as the person's approval, closes no finding and resolves no question. What the agent examined, the agent's assessment with its open questions, and the person's decision are kept and shown apart. Older results keep their origin: an old agent approval is not read as a person's decision; real decisions and earlier reports are kept as they were. This changes the counting rule of the decision of 30 October 2026 (*One rule for what is reviewed*: an approval in a delegated review counted as reviewed by itself); the loop reviewer's acceptance of generated walks stays a separate agent judgement that approves nothing of the code.

**Governing decisions.** VISION.md section 3.4: *"Code accepted" names a review outcome* (23 October; the record keeps who accepted, and delegated agent acceptance does not become acceptance by the user), *Agent-generated code walks* (29 October; a walk or explanation is never an approval), *One rule for what is reviewed* (30 October; now changed as above for the agent's approvals), *Review threads* and *Coverage apart from what remains* (5 November; findings are threads with decisions kept apart from coverage); AGENT_BEHAVIOUR_AND_ORCHESTRATION.md section 5 (an agent's acceptance must not silently become human approval); REVIEW_COVERAGE_AND_CODE_WALKS.md sections 3 and 4 (the user must see the difference between a walk-through, an assessment and a decision); INTERACTION_MODEL.md (discussion is not approval; make clear when the user answers a decision). CODE_WALK_UNDERSTANDING_AND_PROFILES.md is untouched: the explanation profiles' pedagogy is not changed here.

**What is found and built.** (1) The approval was a state fault and a wording fault both: the host records the agent's approved ranges as `agentReviews` with the job's id, and `isHandled` counted such a line as reviewed at once, so the strip, the file percentages and the totals changed with the agent's answer; the wording "reviewed and approved by the agent" then read as a decision. (2) The person's decision over a delegated review is a mark of its own on the job (accept, reject, or withdrawn), kept in the pair record with the other marks through the existing mark channel; only an accepted assessment counts its assessed lines as reviewed through delegation, with the agent named; lines the agent assessed but the person has not decided on are shown as assessed, not as handled, and nothing is invented where the changed lines are not known. (3) The report leads with the state ("Agent review complete, awaiting your assessment", "Accepted by you", "Not accepted"), then what the agent examined and assessed, then what remains (open questions, verification gaps, problems), from the threads. (4) In the walk window the report and the delegation control move into the Step tab's Review section, collapsed, in one place; the step's Review section lists the threads relevant to the step by code place, collapsed, with review-wide ones reachable through the report; a thread is one card per view, the report's lines select it rather than open a second copy. (5) Findings are collapsed by default in every view, open and close independently, and are not reopened by updates; the narrow-panel layout gives the title its own line with the metadata on another. (6) The documents and the delegated-review instruction say the same.

## Implementation Notes

<!-- SECTION:NOTES:BEGIN -->
10 November 2026, Windows, main checkout.

**Root causes.** (1) *The approval was a state fault and a wording fault.* The host wrote the agent's approved ranges as `agentReviews` with the job's id, and `isHandled` (the one rule of 30 October) counted such a line as reviewed at once: the strip went violet, the file percentage and the totals rose, and the walk's hunks read as accepted "by the agent" the moment the job ended. The report then said "N/M changed lines reviewed and approved by the agent", which read as a decision. (2) *The report over Step.* In the walk window the delegation block (`rv-walk-delegate`, with `AgentReviewResult` open by default and a `ThreadIndex` that expanded threads in place) and the thread cards at the place (`threadsHere`, open by default) were rendered above `StepPanel`, so the report and the findings filled the top of Step at every step of the walk, and the same finding stood twice: as a card at the place and as an expanded line of the report's index. (3) *Expansion.* `ThreadCard` opened by default (`open = !closed`), and `ThreadIndex`'s expansion held one id (`useState<string | null>`), so opening a second line closed the first; thread identities themselves were stable (`<job>:f:<hash of the heading>`), so no collision was involved. (4) *Layout.* `.rv-thread-head` was one flex row with the status, kind and reply count unable to shrink (`flex: none`, `nowrap`), so in a narrow panel the title took what was left and wrapped a word a line; the index line added the anchor the same way. The Gerrit view showed the file jobs' reports with the first open and its inline cards open by default.

**Built.** The person's decision about a delegated review is a mark over the job (`PairMark` op `accept-agent`, `reject-agent` or `clear` with a `job` scope), recorded through the existing mark channel with the host checking that the job is a finished review of this pair; `delegationDecisionOf`, `acceptedJobsOf`, `decidedJobsOf`. `LineStatus` now carries the agent's reviews with `accepted` and `awaiting` apart; `isHandled` counts an agent assessment only under an accepted job; the strip has `assessed` (hollow agent colour) beside `agent`; `fileProgressOf` counts `assessed` lines beside handled ones; the walk's `hunkOutcome` reads only accepted assessments. `delegatedReviewView` takes the decision and gives `stateLine`, `examinedLine` (counts of examined and assessed lines against the part's changed lines where known, no figure where not), `attentionLine`, `decision`, `acceptable`, `notAssessed`; the host's job note says "The agent assessed N line(s) as acceptable … awaiting your assessment"; the delegated-review instruction says the "approved" lines are an assessment for the person, not their approval. `AgentReviewResult` is folded by default, leads with the state, then the examined line and what remains from the threads, offers *Accept the agent's assessment*, *Do not accept* and *Withdraw your decision*, and lists its threads one line each that select rather than expand. In the walk window the delegation block moved into the Step tab's Review section (`walk-delegation`), the threads relevant to the step are chosen by the step's places (or the place opened outside the walk) under *The agent's findings at this step*, and a line says how many of the walk's findings are elsewhere in the report. `ThreadCard` folds by default (`defaultOpen` for an opened point), `ThreadIndex` opens lines independently, and the stylesheet gives the head a container query that puts the title on its own line below 34 rem. The simulated provider gained `DSPATCH_REVIEW_SIM=mixed`; `app/scripts/delegation-trial.mjs` is the window trial, added to `linux-verify`'s profiles job; the thread trial's and the scripted scenario's expectations were updated to the new rule.

**Older records.** A record written before this change keeps its `agentReviews` and marks as written; with no job mark, every old delegated approval is the agent's assessment awaiting the person: drawn hollow, counted as nothing, the report saying "awaiting your assessment" with its original date. Nothing is rewritten, no decision is invented, and the person's own marks and earlier reports keep their origin. The trial reproduces this by removing the decision marks from the record between two launches.

**Checks.** `npm run typecheck` clean. Tests: review-pairs, delegated-review-view, review-agent-jobs, review-threads, review-check-threads 109 of 109 (new cases: the counting rule with accept, reject, clear and an older record; a decision through the host's mark channel with its refusals; the view's state, examined and attention lines; the walk's hunk outcome); the review group 349 of 350, the one failure the known Windows path-separator case in `review-context.test.ts`. The delegation trial on Windows: 51 entries, all passed; screenshots and results in `docs/evaluations/dsp-193/`. Not run here: the thread trial and the scripted `code-walks` scenario (Linux and xdotool; the thread trial runs in CI's dock job).

**Not established.** Anything with a real provider in this task; the panel docked in the diff window at a narrow width (the trial docks in the walk window and reads the floating panel narrow).
